The vocabulary consists of common linguistic facts and short glosses, rather than reproduced articles or lessons. Original teaching notes avoid claims about disputed origin histories or universal cultural practices. The words Haalu (milk) and Hallu (teeth) intentionally remain separate. The example name Bhoja is retained from the source; families can practise a personal introduction with a speaker.

The source includes alternate Roman spellings, including for numbers. The starter retains the selected source form rather than generating alternatives. Family recordings may preserve local or household forms. They are saved locally and do not alter the shared curriculum.

Before adding material, record its provenance, verify usage with a fluent Badaga speaker, and confirm permission to redistribute any recordings, photographs, stories or dictionary material. A code repository’s licence does not establish the rights to a separately linked dataset or audio collection.

The game trail and founder artwork are imaginative illustrations. The landscape is inspired by the Nilgiri Hills, not a factual map or a claim about a specific village. Founder scenes are conceptual and other people are fictional.
