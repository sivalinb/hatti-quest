# Five-minute product demo

[Watch on Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RO7AD9YobbUAH80qbrk8_ZS6I47WXXio/view) (anyone with the link can view), or [download the five-minute MP4](demo/hatti-quest-five-minute-demo.mp4). [Captions](demo/captions.srt), [chaptered transcript](demo/transcript.md), [narration timings](demo/narration.json).

The video is 300 seconds, 1280×720, H.264/AAC with captions burned into the picture. It uses eleven recorded sections and synthetic English narration. The file is included in the public repository for a self-contained demo.

The demo combines conceptual illustrations with recordings of the working local Python application, live Story Studio, AI evidence dashboard and provisioned Grafana dashboard. A synthetic English presenter tells Siva Babu's story in the third person, using the identity and purpose he supplied. The narration does not clone or claim to be his voice. The founder card also presents him in the third person.

The 4 October 2026 revision applies Siva's requested pronunciation cues: **Badaga → “baduuugaa”** and **Nilgiris → “neel-giri-s”**. Synthesis uses the phonetic respellings `ba-dooo-gaa` and `neel-giri-s`; captions and the transcript retain the standard names. A short synthesis/transcription check heard “baduuga” and “neel giris”. This is a check of English presenter production, rather than certification of native Badaga pronunciation. [Pronunciation preprocessing background](https://deepgram.com/learn/common-tts-pronunciation-errors).

The Nilgiri illustration is storybook scenery rather than documentary footage or a geographical reconstruction. Siva's illustration uses his authorised public profile likeness. Other people and scenes are fictional.

## Narrative and fact sources

- Nilgiri context and regional communities: [Nilgiris District](https://nilgiris.nic.in/about-district/), [UNESCO material describing six Nilgiri indigenous communities](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/00168-EN.pdf). The introduction names Toda, Kota, Kurumba, Irula, Paniya and Kattunayaka without equating all regional communities with a legal tribal classification.
- Badaga's Dravidian classification and the dated count: [Linguistic Survey of India: Tamil Nadu](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/45244/download/48951/LSI_TAMIL_NADU.pdf), [2011 Census speakers' abstract](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/42458/download/46089/C-16_25062018.pdf). The narration gives 133,550 Badaga mother-tongue speakers in India in 2011, not a current worldwide population estimate.
- Writing systems: [community script discussion](https://badaga.co/2019/08/09/a-script-for-badaga/) and [Paul Hockings' correspondence](https://badaga.co/2013/06/25/badaga-the-language/). The narration distinguishes an oral tradition and lack of one broadly standardised native orthography from the ability to write Badaga using existing and proposed systems.
- Food, village life and the connection to land: [community food and recipes](https://badaga.co/recipes/) and [Badaga community traditions](https://badaga.co/2013/03/04/badagas-in-a-nutshell/). These provide context for the founder's motivation; the illustrations remain conceptual.
- Vocabulary: the source records linked from every word card. No third-party archive recording or song is used in the video.
- AI claims: the checked-in [live evaluation report](../evals/reports/latest.json) and actual provider traces, rather than simulated integration output.

The diaspora problem and proposed solution reflect the founder's stated motivation. The app is a working prototype with pending fluent speaker review, rather than a demonstrated intervention with measured learning outcomes.

Narration uses Deepgram Aura-2's English voice. It is a production asset, not Badaga text-to-speech for children. [Deepgram TTS documentation](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/text-to-speech).
